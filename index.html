The Great Conversion

The Factor-Label Method (Dimensional Analysis)

Because different situations call for different unit sizes, physicists must convert measurements without changing the actual quantity. The safest way is the factor-label method.

  1. Write down the given measurement.
  2. Multiply by a conversion factor (a fraction equal to 1).
  3. Arrange so unwanted units cancel.
  4. Multiply straight across.
k×1000
h×100
da×10
m / g / L×1
d×0.1
c×0.01
m×0.001

Case Study: The $125 Million Mistake

In 1999, NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter because one team used pound-force seconds (English units) while the navigation software expected newton-seconds (SI units).

The navigation software was fed thruster-force numbers that were roughly 4.45 times smaller than the real value!

Multiplication

Multiply coefficients, ADD exponents.

(3.0 × 104) (2.0 × 103) = 6.0 × 107

Division

Divide coefficients, SUBTRACT exponents.

(8.0 × 108) ÷ (2.0 × 103) = 4.0 × 105

Truth & Honor

Accuracy vs. Precision

Accuracy

How close a measurement is to the true or accepted value.

A scale that always reads 2kg heavier is precise, but not accurate.

Precision

How close repeated measurements are to one another.

A scale that gives random readings every time is accurate on average, but not precise.

Significant Figures

Digits that carry real meaning and reflect the precision of the instrument.

  • 1 All non-zero digits are significant. (245 = 3 sig figs)
  • 2 Zeros between non-zero digits are significant. (2005 = 4 sig figs)
  • 3 Leading zeros are never significant. (0.0025 = 2 sig figs)
  • 4 Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant. (2.500 = 4 sig figs)

Percent Error

Percent Error = |Experimental Value - Accepted Value| ÷ Accepted Value × 100%

If water boils at 98.5°C (accepted is 100°C): |98.5 - 100| ÷ 100 × 100% = 1.5% error
