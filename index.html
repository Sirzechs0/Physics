Fundamental Quantity

Cannot be defined in terms of any other physical quantity. The SI system recognizes seven: length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.

Derived Quantity

Built by combining two or more fundamental quantities, such as speed (length ÷ time) or density (mass ÷ volume).

SI Unit

The standardized unit for a quantity under the International System of Units, chosen so measurements can be compared anywhere in the world.

Factor-Label Method

Also called dimensional analysis. Convert a measurement by multiplying it by conversion factors — fractions equal to 1 — arranged so the unwanted units cancel out.

Scientific Notation

Writes very large or very small numbers as a coefficient between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of 10 — for example, 4.5 × 10⁷.

Accuracy

How close a measurement is to the true or accepted value.

Precision

How close repeated measurements are to one another, regardless of whether they're close to the true value.

Significant Figures

The digits in a measurement that carry real meaning and reflect the precision of the instrument that produced them.

Percent Error

|Experimental − Accepted| ÷ Accepted × 100% — a measure of how far a result strays from the accepted answer.

The Great Conversion

The Factor-Label Method (Dimensional Analysis)

Because different situations call for different unit sizes, physicists must convert measurements without changing the actual quantity. The safest way is the factor-label method.

  1. Write down the given measurement.
  2. Multiply by a conversion factor (a fraction equal to 1).
  3. Arrange so unwanted units cancel.
  4. Multiply straight across.

Truth & Honor

Accuracy vs. Precision

Accuracy

How close a measurement is to the true or accepted value.

A scale that always reads 2kg heavier is precise, but not accurate.

Precision

How close repeated measurements are to one another.

A scale that gives random readings every time is accurate on average, but not precise.

Significant Figures

Digits that carry real meaning and reflect the precision of the instrument.

  • 1 All non-zero digits are significant. (245 = 3 sig figs)
  • 2 Zeros between non-zero digits are significant. (2005 = 4 sig figs)
  • 3 Leading zeros are never significant. (0.0025 = 2 sig figs)
  • 4 Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant. (2.500 = 4 sig figs)

Percent Error

Percent Error = |Experimental Value - Accepted Value| ÷ Accepted Value × 100%

If water boils at 98.5°C (accepted is 100°C): |98.5 - 100| ÷ 100 × 100% = 1.5% error

The Measurement Investigation Mission

Goal

Investigate a real, local measurement scenario and communicate whether the measurement involved can be trusted — applying fundamental/derived quantities, unit conversion, scientific notation, and an evaluation of accuracy and precision.

Role

You are a Field Metrology Officer — a measurement quality inspector — for a community-based organization.

Audience

Barangay officials, fellow students, and community members attending a "Measurement Matters" community exhibit.

Situation

Your community is launching a public exhibit called "Getting It Right: Why Measurement Matters", meant to help the public understand how accurate measurement protects health, safety, and fairness in everyday transactions.

Product / Performance

Create a Measurement Investigation Report and Infographic/Poster that includes:

  • Title and chosen scenario
  • The physical quantity investigated, and whether it is fundamental or derived
  • A data table of at least three trial measurements
  • Unit conversions performed (at least two different units)
  • Scientific notation, where appropriate
  • An evaluation of accuracy (percent error against an accepted or reference value) and precision (consistency across trials)
  • A real-world recommendation based on the findings
  • At least two credible references, cited in APA 7th Edition format
